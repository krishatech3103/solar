How Solar Energy Works

From Sunlight to Your Wall Sockets.

Rooftop solar is an elegant, solid-state technology with no moving parts. Follow the energy path below or click any step to see how electricity is generated and consumed.

Step 1: Abundant Sunlight in Maharashtra

Western Maharashtra receives over 300 days of bright sunshine annually. Solar photovoltaic cells do not need scorching heat—they convert natural ambient light photons directly into a steady electrical current throughout the day.

Step 2: High-Efficiency Solar PV Panels

Monocrystalline silicon panels installed on your rooftop capture daylight and initiate the photovoltaic reaction, creating Direct Current (DC) electricity silently without any fuel or carbon emissions.

Step 3: Smart Solar Inverter Conversion

Household lights, refrigerators, motors, and commercial computers run on Alternating Current (AC). The wall-mounted solar inverter efficiently transforms raw DC electricity into clean, synchronized 230V/415V AC electricity suitable for your appliances.

Step 4: Immediate Daytime Consumption

Electricity follows the path of least resistance. The power generated by your rooftop panels is consumed first by whatever appliances are running in your building—reducing the electricity you need to draw from the utility provider.

Step 5: Bi-directional Net Metering with the Grid

When your solar system produces more electricity than your property is using, the surplus automatically flows backward through a DISCOM-approved bi-directional net meter into the electricity grid, earning kilowatt-hour credits against nighttime usage.

System Architecture

Choosing the Right Solar System Type.

Different properties have different power supply characteristics. We help you choose the configuration that best balances cost, simplicity, and backup requirements.

Most Popular

On-Grid Solar

Best for areas with stable electricity supply

  • Connected directly to the utility grid
  • Utilizes bi-directional net metering
  • Lowest upfront investment per kilowatt
  • No costly battery bank replacement cycles
  • Shuts down during grid power cuts for safety

Hybrid Solar

Solar + Grid + Emergency Battery Backup

  • Synchronizes with grid net metering
  • Automatically stores excess power in batteries
  • Keeps essential loads running during outages
  • Seamless switchover for sensitive electronics
  • Higher initial investment due to battery storage

Off-Grid Solar

Independent power for remote or farm locations

  • Fully independent of the electric utility
  • Heavy-duty battery storage bank
  • Ideal for farmhouses, pumps & remote sheds
  • Zero dependency on DISCOM approvals

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions.

Helpful answers to common questions about rooftop solar sizing, net metering, cloudy weather, and maintenance.

System size depends primarily on your average monthly consumption and available shadow-free rooftop space. A typical home consuming 250 to 350 units a month usually requires an estimated 3 kW system, whereas homes with multiple air conditioners and heavy pumps often plan for 5 kW or more. A physical site survey helps pinpoint the optimal capacity.
As a practical benchmark, modern solar panels require roughly 80 to 100 square feet of shadow-free terrace area per kilowatt (kW) of capacity. An elevated structure can be built so that the terrace floor remains accessible for sitting, drying clothes, or gardening underneath.
A properly designed rooftop solar system can offset a significant portion of your daytime electricity consumption. Surplus electricity produced during peak sunlight hours is exported to the grid via a net meter, earning credits that offset the electricity you draw at night.
Yes. Solar panels generate power from ambient daylight, not heat. On cloudy or rainy days, generation continues but at reduced output (typically 20% to 40% of peak sunny day production). Your property automatically draws any additional power needed seamlessly from the utility grid.
On-Grid systems connect directly to the utility grid with net metering and do not contain batteries; they shut down during a grid outage to prevent backfeeding into the lines. Hybrid systems combine grid connection with battery storage, allowing essential loads to keep running even when the electricity grid is down.
Standard on-grid solar systems automatically turn off during grid power cuts for safety reasons (anti-islanding protection). If you experience frequent load shedding and need power during blackouts, a hybrid solar system with dedicated battery backup or a compatible solar inverter is recommended.
The physical rooftop installation of mounting structures, panels, inverters, and cabling typically takes 2 to 4 days for residential systems (3 kW–10 kW). Net metering approval, DISCOM inspection, and bi-directional meter installation generally require additional time according to local utility timelines.
Solar PV systems have no moving mechanical parts, which keeps maintenance very minimal. The primary task is periodically cleaning dust and bird droppings off the glass surface with plain water every 10 to 15 days to maintain maximum light absorption.
Yes. Commercial establishments and industrial factories often achieve excellent solar economics because their peak operating hours align directly with daylight sun hours, allowing them to directly offset expensive commercial tariffs.
Government solar schemes, portal guidelines, and subsidy eligibility criteria change over time based on central and state policy updates. Current eligibility and documentation requirements should be verified against the latest official government portal rules before installation.
